The Blue-Sky Ceiling

You asked whether blue sky is just the all-time high, and whether the desk was giving up too early. The line on your board named the wrong reason. It said the retained history was not split-adjusted. Every row of it is. It was refusing on something smaller and fixable — and the all-time high, which is knowable, is still not the number you want on these names.

What the ceiling actually is

The blue-sky level is the highest prior-session high in the retained window, and nothing more. It is not an all-time high and not a 52-week high, and it is never described as one. Today is excluded on purpose: overhead supply means levels established before now, and today's own high is the move being assessed rather than a ceiling over it.

The four things that can make a ceiling a lie

Each has a guard, and any of them leaves the level unverified rather than shown.

no history Unavailable, not open sky. Nothing retained for the name. A ceiling nobody stored is not a ceiling of zero.
under 40 sessions Too shallow to mean anything. A name with nine sessions on file is trivially at its highest. That is a fact about the cache, not about the stock.
high over 1.6× its close Reads as a bad print. A genuine blow-off closes somewhere near its range. A spike that towers over the close of its own session and leaves nothing behind sets a ceiling nothing ever traded at.
fewer than 2 within 10% An isolated legacy spike. One day nothing else came near is not a shelf of supply. The ceiling has to be corroborated before it is trusted.

Why a quarter of your names refused

A fifth condition sits in front of those four: the retained window has to sit on one adjustment basis. A price from before a 1-for-5 reverse split and a price from after it are not the same number, and a ceiling drawn across both is wrong by the split ratio.

The desk could not vouch for that, so it refused — and the sentence it refused with was about the wrong thing.

Measured 2026-09-22. Every row of daily_bars in both stores is labelled split-adjusted — 7,173 of 7,173 on the local store and 24,429 of 24,429 in the 2026-08-23 production snapshot. None are raw.

What actually triggered the refusal was a symbol holding rows from more than one fetch date — 68 of 256 symbols, about 27%, in that same snapshot. “Split-adjusted as of August” and “split-adjusted as of September” are different bases under one word, so the desk refused every name holding two. That was a guess about what could have happened, not a finding about what did.

The desk now asks the narrower question. A split factor is the cumulative adjustment still to apply at a date, so it can only fall as the date advances — a later session has fewer splits after it. When a factor rises instead, two rows were adjusted on different bases, and that is the stale row showing itself: written before a 1-for-5 and never restated, it carries 1 where its neighbour carries 5, and its stored price is wrong by the same five. That is the defect seen directly rather than guessed at.

And the all-time high is still not the number you want

It is knowable. The desk can already reach ten years back, in two vendor requests per name, and it does exactly that for a top-ten name whose price has cleared everything on file. Here is what full depth returns for BTCT.

$14,249.94 All-history ceiling. A real session, arithmetically correct — and it traded four shares. factor 600 · 2 consolidations
——— not to scale, and it cannot be ———
$26.58 Highest prior high on today's basis. The deepest level that is still the same kind of share you would be buying. factor 1 · 2024-11-13
$1.71 Where it traded when this was reported on the live board, against a $1.48 retained ceiling. factor 1 · 2026-08-24

The top rung is not wrong. It is unreadable. Blue sky means no trapped holders above — nobody waiting to get out even, no shelf where the last advance died. A price restated through two consolidations describes shares that no longer exist in that form. There is no supply at $14,249.94, because there is nobody there.

So the desk carries both: the deep ceiling with its factor beside it, and the highest prior high that sits on today's basis. On BTCT the verdict is the same either way — $26.58 is still ten times the price — but only one of those is a number you can act on.

What is still switched off

The narrower question can only be asked where a split basis has actually been established, and establishing one is a separate job: two vendor requests per name, once. Until that has run, a name fetched on two dates still refuses — it now says why, accurately, but it still refuses. A basis covering only part of a window does not count either, because a clean subset would otherwise vouch for rows nothing examined.
