WHAT IT DOES THAT A CHART DOES NOT

None of this is on the chart.

01 — IT CATCHES THEM COMING INTO PLAY

As it happens, not once it tops a list

Relative volume is ranked across every small cap at once, so a name shows up as it crosses into the day's runners, not after it is already third on somebody's list. Once it is on the board it stays for the session, because the one you stopped watching is the one that runs.

02 — IT READS THE FILINGS. YOU DON’T HAVE TO.

Nobody is typing a ticker into EDGAR at 07:15

Not with half a decision already made and the name still moving. The filings were pulled and read when the name reached the top of the ranking. How, and by what rules, is below.

03 — IT SAYS CAPACITY.

It never says selling.

A resale registration, a warrant, a convertible: each is labelled as what it is, shares that can come to market. Not shares that are coming. A filing tells you capacity and nothing else.

04 — IT TELLS YOU THE FLOAT IS WRONG

DIRTY FLOAT

The float on your screen is a number someone published on a date. It knows nothing about the shelf that went effective on Tuesday, the warrants struck under the current price, or the note that converts at a discount to here.

The displayed float understates the shares that can reach the market.

THE RESEARCH IS DONE BEFORE YOU SIT DOWN

The filings get read the night before. Not because you asked.

A name that moves after the bell is on the desk's list that evening. The desk finds the company behind the ticker, pulls the filings that can carry a share count, a registration, a resale or a financing, and reads the paragraphs that bear on the question. Not the most recent twenty documents: a shelf that went effective in March does not get pushed off the list by a run of 8-Ks.

By the time the premarket opens, each name at the top of the ranking carries a reading, or says plainly that it does not have one yet.

And it does not read again on a timer. A name is re-read when the company files something, not because ten minutes passed.

FOUR RULES IT READS BY

Shares outstanding is never called float.

A count off a cover page is an upper bound on the float, and it is labelled as one. Every share an insider holds is inside it.

Supply is two numbers, not one.

What is proven live, and what is filed at most. The gap between them is the desk's uncertainty, printed as a width. It is never rounded to zero.

A count from before a reverse split is never compared to one from after it.

A block counts against today's shares only when the desk knows both were stated on the same basis. Unknown is not yes.

Every float is somebody’s computation. No filing we know of reports the current number of tradable shares.

So the desk checks the float it is shown against the filed share count, and refuses the one that cannot be right. The rules are visible and a failure is detectable.

“Not researched” and “researched, nothing found” are different states and they look different on the board. The first says the desk has not looked. The second says it looked.

WHY IT EXISTS

The move is obvious. What is underneath it is not.

By the time a small cap clears your scanner it is already running and you are deciding in seconds. Nothing on the chart says there is an ATM live under it, that the float on your screen is the one from before the offering, or that this is day two and day one already failed.

All of it is public. All of it is filed. None of it is findable in the time you actually have. In August it was 50% movers, one after another, on nothing anyone could name at 07:15. This was built to close that gap, by someone who trades it, focused on the premarket and the early regular session.

Premarket, regular hours or after the close, the question is the same: what is this move standing on? The board runs from 04:00 through the after-hours session, and a float does not get cleaner at 09:30.

HOW IT WORKS

The Momentum Intelligence Trading Desk is built for people who trade $1–$20 small-cap momentum in the premarket. It is not a scanner and it is not a signal service: it takes the names that are already moving and puts everything the desk knows about each one on a single shared board — why it is moving, where it sits in the cycle, what the share structure is, and what supply stands behind it. Where it has nothing, it says so.

The tape and the paperwork. Kept apart on purpose.

One reads what the market is doing. One reads what the company has filed. They disagree more often than you would think, and the desk shows you both instead of blending them into a score.
