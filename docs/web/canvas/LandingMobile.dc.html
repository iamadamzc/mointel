WHAT IT DOES THAT A CHART DOES NOT

Four things, and the fourth is the one that costs people money.

01 — IT CATCHES THEM COMING INTO PLAY

As it happens, not once it tops a list

Relative volume is ranked across every small cap at once, so a name shows up as it crosses into the day's runners, not after it is already third on somebody's list. Once it is on the board it stays for the session, because the one you stopped watching is the one that runs.

02 — IT READS THE FILINGS BEFORE YOU ASK

The structure work is done before the premarket is

When a name reaches the top of the ranking its filings are pulled and read, on a schedule that starts before the premarket does. Nobody is typing a ticker into EDGAR at 07:15 with half a decision already made.

03 — IT SAYS CAPACITY.

It never says selling.

A resale registration, a warrant, a convertible: each is labelled as what it is, shares that can come to market. Not shares that are coming. A filing tells you capacity and nothing else.

04 — IT TELLS YOU THE FLOAT IS WRONG

DIRTY FLOAT

The float on your screen is a number someone published on a date. It knows nothing about the shelf that went effective on Tuesday, the warrants struck under the current price, or the note that converts at a discount to here.

The displayed float understates the shares that can reach the market.

WHY IT EXISTS

The move is obvious. What is underneath it is not.

By the time a small cap clears your scanner it is already running and you are deciding in seconds. Nothing on the chart says there is an ATM live under it, that the float on your screen is the one from before the offering, or that this is day two and day one already failed.

All of it is public. All of it is filed. None of it is findable in the time you actually have. This was built to close that gap, by someone who trades it, at the desk he trades it from.

HOW IT WORKS

TMI Trader is an intelligence desk for people who trade $1–$20 small-cap momentum in the premarket. It is not a scanner and it is not a signal service: it takes the names that are already moving and puts everything the desk knows about each one on a single shared board — why it is moving, where it sits in the cycle, what the share structure is, and what supply stands behind it. Where it has nothing, it says so.

The tape and the paperwork. Kept apart on purpose.

One reads what the market is doing. One reads what the company has filed. They disagree more often than you would think, and the desk shows you both instead of blending them into a score.

DEMAND — THE TAPE

What the market is doing

Volume against the name's own twenty-session baseline, the ranking that follows from it, and the moment a name crosses into the day's runners.

STRUCTURE — THE PAPERWORK

What that move is standing on

Float, and whether the float can be trusted. Warrants, convertibles, PIPEs, ATMs, resale shelves, reverse-split distortion. The levels, and the warnings attached to them.

WHEN THERE IS NOTHING TO SAY

Unknown stays unknown

Nothing missing is rounded to zero, and nothing is asserted that the source did not state. Where the research has nothing, the desk prints nothing and says so.
