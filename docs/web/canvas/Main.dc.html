WHAT IT DOES THAT A CHART DOES NOT

Four things, and the fourth is the one that costs people money.

01 — IT FINDS THEM JOINING

Cohort entry, not a leaderboard

Relative volume is ranked across the whole tape at once, so a name is caught crossing into the day's cohort rather than after it is already top of a list. And once it qualifies it stays on the board for the session — a name that cools at 08:10 and goes again at 09:20 is still there, because the thing you stop watching is the thing that runs.

02 — IT READS THE FILINGS UNPROMPTED

The structure work happens before you ask

A name reaching the top of the ranking has its filings pulled and classified automatically, on a schedule that starts before the premarket does. You are not typing a ticker into a filings search at 07:15. The work is already done, or the board says plainly that it is not.

03 — IT SEPARATES CAPACITY FROM INTENT

It says capacity, never selling

Registered resale, warrant and conversion capacity is labelled as capacity, because capacity is what a filing actually establishes. Nobody can read a filing and tell you someone is selling — a product that says they are is guessing, and you will find out which on the day it matters.

04 — IT TELLS YOU THE FLOAT IS WRONG

DIRTY FLOAT

The float on your screen is a number someone published on a date. It does not know about the shelf that went effective on Tuesday, the warrants struck below the current price, or the note that converts at a discount to where it is trading now.

When those exist, the board says so in one label and one sentence: the displayed float understates the shares that can reach the market.

WHY IT EXISTS

The move is obvious. What is underneath it is not.

By the time a small cap clears a scanner it is already running, and you are deciding in seconds. Nothing on the chart tells you there is an ATM live underneath it, that the float on your screen is the one from before the offering, or that this is day two and day one already failed.

All of it is public. All of it is filed. None of it is findable in the time you actually have — and that gap is the only thing this was built to close.

Built by someone trading it, on the desk he trades it from, between 07:00 and 10:00.

HOW IT WORKS

TMI Trader is an intelligence desk for people who trade $1–$20 small-cap momentum in the premarket. It is not a scanner and it is not a signal service: it takes the names that are already moving and puts everything the desk knows about each one on a single shared board — why it is moving, where it sits in the cycle, what the share structure is, and what supply stands behind it. Where it has nothing, it says so.

Two engines. Deliberately never merged.

One reads the tape. One reads the filings. They answer different questions, they disagree often, and the desk shows both rather than combining them into one number.

DEMAND — THE TAPE

What the market is doing

A continuous read of the market, held open all session. Volume against a twenty-session baseline, the ranking that follows from it, and the moment a name crosses into the day's discovery list.

STRUCTURE — THE PAPERWORK

What that move is standing on

Float, and whether the float can be trusted. Supply waiting in the wings: warrants, convertibles, PIPEs, ATMs, resale shelves, reverse-split distortion. The levels that matter and the warnings attached to them.

WHEN THERE IS NOTHING TO SAY

Unknown stays unknown

No missing value is quietly rounded to zero, and no classification is asserted that the source did not state. When the tape has found something and the research has nothing to say about it, the desk prints exactly that and leaves the column empty.
