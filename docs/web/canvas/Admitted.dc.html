OPEN

Where refusal happens decides which screen exists

lib/auth/store.ts:57 says it plainly: the column exists, nothing reads it, and where it gets read is not decided. That is not an implementation detail — it decides which of two screens a refused person ever sees.

Refuse at the callback and no session is issued: the person lands back on the sign-in flow, and every later visit re-runs Google to be refused again. Refuse at the point of use and they hold a session, so this screen can tell them where they stand without making them authenticate to hear it.

Joint recommendation, Web and Design, 2026-09-10: refuse at the point of use — provided the status check lives in ONE place that every point of use passes through. One session-resolution function, or one middleware. Scattered per-route checks and the callback gate is the safer choice instead: point-of-use multiplies the places that must refuse, and missing one hands a pending user the board. That condition is Design's and it is load-bearing — without it the recommendation flips.

The price, stated because it is real and Design has accepted it: point-of-use makes the mid-session downgrade common rather than rare, since every refused person holds a live session. So the board's downgrade behaviour has to exist before enforcement ships, not after.

DESIGN'S, NOT DRAWN HERE: a session revoked mid-morning is a freshness fault, not an access fault. The board does not blank and does not silently freeze — at 07:30 he may be in a position, and taking away the only thing telling him what he is in is worse than what the revocation was protecting. Every number carries its staleness loudly, and the way back is offered. Never a redirect, never a modal.
