SETTLED

Refusal happens at the point of use

His ruling, 2026-09-11. A refused person holds a session, so these three screens are reachable and are the whole of what they see. The sign-in flow does not refuse anyone; it creates the row and succeeds.

The condition Design attached is part of the ruling, not a footnote: the status check lives in ONE place every point of use passes through — one session-resolution function, or one middleware. Point-of-use multiplies the places that must refuse; a scattered check hands a pending user the board, and that defect is invisible from inside any single route.

And the price is now owed: every refused person holds a live session, so the mid-session downgrade is common rather than rare. DESIGN'S, AND NOW OBLIGATORY BEFORE ENFORCEMENT SHIPS: a revoked session is a freshness fault, not an access fault. The board does not blank and does not silently freeze — at 07:30 he may be in a position, and taking away the only thing telling him what he is in is worse than what the revocation was protecting.
